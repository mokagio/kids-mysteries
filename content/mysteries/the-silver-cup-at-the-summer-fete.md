At three o'clock the Bexley Silver Cup was to be handed to whoever had grown the finest marrow.

The vicar polished the cup, set it on the trophy table, and stood beside it chatting to the judges until **five past two**.
When he came back at **half past two**, the table was bare.

The trophy table sits at the top of the field, and there is only one way up to it: across the running track, which the groundsman had marked out that afternoon with wet white line-paint.

{{< clues >}}

1. The line-paint was laid at two o'clock and took an hour to dry. Anyone who went up to the trophy table had to walk across it.
2. The ice-cream van, the face-painting stall and the tea tent are all at the bottom of the field — a five-minute walk from the trophy table, and five minutes back.
3. Percy's van prints the time on every sale. The till roll shows ice creams sold at 2:06, 2:11, 2:17, 2:22 and 2:27.
4. Nell's tiger takes twenty minutes to paint. She started at 2:06, and the boy's mother photographed the finished tiger at 2:26.
5. The vicar sat in the tea tent from five past two until half past, drinking three cups and talking to Dot the whole while. A dozen people in the queue watched the pair of them.
6. A line of white footprints leads from the running track up to the trophy table — and away again, down to the steps of the bandstand.
7. A photograph of the band taken at 2:20 shows every player in place, and the conductor's podium empty.

{{< /clues >}}

{{< solution culprit="Arthur Small" >}}

The cup was still on the table at 2:05 and gone by 2:30, so the thief needed ten clear minutes inside that half hour — five up the field and five back.

Percy never had them: he sold an ice cream every five or six minutes right through.
Nell was painting her tiger from 2:06 until 2:26, and four minutes is not enough to walk ten minutes.
Dot had the vicar talking at her from 2:05 to 2:30, so she never left the tea tent either.

That leaves Arthur Small, who says he never stepped off his podium — but the photograph taken at 2:20 shows the podium empty while the band played on.
A band doesn't stop just because the conductor slips away for one tune.
And the white footprints lead back to the bandstand steps.

Arthur's own marrow, by the way, came second. The cup was going to Mrs. Bunce.
