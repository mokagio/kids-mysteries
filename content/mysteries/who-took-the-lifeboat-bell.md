The brass bell hangs above the door of the Puddle Bay lifeboat station.
It is rung when the boat goes out.

It was there when the station opened at **ten o'clock**. By **noon** it was gone.

The bell is heavy — as heavy as a big dog. Nobody could carry it far in their arms.
And the morning tide had washed the beach smooth, so anything that crossed the sand left a mark.

All three of these people were on the beach that morning, and each one has an {{< word def="a reason why you could not have done it, because you were somewhere else at the time" >}}alibi{{< /word >}}.
Two of them are telling the truth.

{{< suspect name="Ned Salt" role="The Fisherman" emoji="🎣" >}}
"I was mending my nets by the boat. I never went near the station."
{{< /suspect >}}

{{< suspect name="Pip Dowd" role="The Ice-Cream Seller" emoji="🍦" >}}
"I was at my van by the sea wall all morning."
{{< /suspect >}}

{{< suspect name="Mrs. Larkin" role="The Donkey Lady" emoji="🐴" >}}
"I was down on the sand with the donkeys the whole time."
{{< /suspect >}}

{{< /suspects >}}

{{< clues >}}

1. One set of wheel tracks crosses the smooth sand, from the station door to the sea wall steps. It is a single narrow line.
2. Ned's fish barrow has two wide wheels. It sat above the tide line all morning with no track behind it.
3. Mrs. Larkin's donkey cart has two wide wheels. Its tracks run along the water's edge and never come near the station.
4. A bicycle pushed along the sand leaves one narrow line, because the back wheel runs in the track of the front one.
5. Pip's bicycle has a big basket on the front, and there is a smear of brass polish inside it.

{{< /clues >}}

{{< solution culprit="Pip Dowd" >}}

The track is the whole case. One narrow line, from the station door to the sea wall steps and back.

Ned's barrow has two wide wheels, and it never moved off the tide line.

Mrs. Larkin's cart has two wide wheels too, and its tracks stay down at the water's edge.

One narrow line is what a bicycle makes when you push it, because the back wheel follows the front one.

Pip has a bicycle with a big basket, and there is brass polish inside the basket.
She said she never left her van — but her bicycle went across that sand and came back.

Her {{< word def="the reason someone had for doing it" >}}motive{{< /word >}} was sitting on the van all morning: her own bell had cracked in half, and without a bell nobody knew the ice cream had come.

The lifeboat bell is back above the door. Pip has bought herself a new one, and rings it twice as loud.

{{< /solution >}}
