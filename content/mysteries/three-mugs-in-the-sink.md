Arthur Coll looked after Puddle Bay Museum at night.

Every evening at **nine o'clock** he made cocoa — one mug for himself, and one for anybody still in the building.

On Tuesday morning he was found at the foot of the museum stairs.
His watch had broken as he fell. It had stopped at **ten past nine**.

Three people were in the museum that evening, and each one has an {{< word def="a reason why you could not have done it, because you were somewhere else at the time" >}}alibi{{< /word >}}.
Two of them are telling the truth.

{{< clues >}}

1. There were three mugs in the sink.
2. Elsie was on the eight o'clock bus. The driver is her {{< word def="somebody who saw what happened and can tell you about it" >}}witness{{< /word >}} — he remembers her, because she was carrying a birthday cake.
3. At ten past nine, Gus was down at the harbour, signing his name in the harbourmaster's book for a mooring rope.
4. One of the three mugs had a smear of green paint on the handle.
5. Rosa had spent the whole evening touching up a cracked display case with green paint.

{{< /clues >}}

{{< solution culprit="Rosa Pell" >}}

Arthur always made one mug for himself.
So three mugs means **two** other people were in the museum at nine o'clock — not one.

Gus is one of them. He says so himself.

The other one is not Elsie. She was on the eight o'clock bus, a whole hour before the cocoa was made, and the driver remembers her.

And it was not Gus who was still there at ten past nine. His name is in the harbourmaster's book at that very minute, down at the harbour.

That leaves Rosa, who says she left at half past eight, before the cocoa was made.
But one of the three mugs has green paint on the handle, and Rosa had been painting all evening.

She was still in the museum at ten past nine.
Arthur had come up the stairs and seen the crack in the case — the crack she had made, and had spent all evening painting over. She pushed past him, and he fell.

{{< /solution >}}
