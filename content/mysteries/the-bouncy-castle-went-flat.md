Every year the Barrow Green show has a bouncy castle, and every year it is the best thing there.

This year it sagged in the middle of the afternoon.
By the time the last child slid off, it was lying flat on the grass.

The man from the castle company checked every seam, twice.
Then he went round the back, where nobody goes, and looked at the pump.

Three grown-ups were near the castle that afternoon.
Each one says they were somewhere else.
That is called an {{< word def="a reason why you could not have done it, because you were somewhere else at the time" >}}alibi{{< /word >}}.

Two of them are telling the truth.

{{< suspect name="Nan Pike" role="The Raffle Lady" emoji="🎟️" >}}
"I was calling the raffle. I did not stop all afternoon."
{{< /suspect >}}

{{< suspect name="Wilf Slee" role="The Farmer" emoji="🚜" >}}
"I was in the sack race, in the far field."
{{< /suspect >}}

{{< suspect name="Vi Marsh" role="The Tea Lady" emoji="☕" >}}
"I was in my tea tent. I saw the castle go down from there."
{{< /suspect >}}

{{< /suspects >}}

{{< clues >}}

1. There was no hole in the bouncy castle. The pump had been switched off at the plug, round the back.
2. Nan Pike's voice came out of the loudspeaker all afternoon. The microphone stands at the bandstand, right across the field from the castle.
3. Wilf Slee spent the whole race in the far field, tied into a sack. Forty people watched him do it, and every one of them is a {{< word def="somebody who saw what happened and can tell you about it" >}}witness{{< /word >}}.
4. You cannot see the bouncy castle from the tea tent. A tall hedge stands between them.
5. Everybody said the castle must have a hole in it. One person said the pump had been switched off — and said it before anybody had been round the back to look.

{{< /clues >}}

{{< solution culprit="Vi Marsh" >}}

Nan Pike was talking into the microphone at the bandstand, right across the field.
Her voice never stopped, so she never walked anywhere.

Wilf Slee was tied into a sack in the far field, with forty people watching.
Nobody switches off a pump in a sack.

That leaves Vi Marsh, and Vi says two things that cannot both be true.
She says she watched the castle go down from the tea tent.
But there is a tall hedge in the way, and from the tea tent you cannot see the castle at all.

Then there is the pump.
Everybody at the show thought the castle had a hole in it, because that is what a flat castle looks like.
Only one person said it had been switched off, and she said it while the castle man was still checking the seams.
She could only know that if she had done it herself.

The castle had been put up right in front of her tea tent, and all day nobody could see her sign.
Vi Marsh sold nine cups of tea, and that is what made her the {{< word def="the person who did it" >}}culprit{{< /word >}}.

{{< /solution >}}
