The show at the Regal finished at **half past six**.

High above the stage there is a narrow walkway, and along it hang heavy sandbags on ropes.
They hold the scenery steady.

At **half past seven**, Mr Pym the stage manager was found lying on the stage with a sandbag across him.
Everybody looked up at the walkway and said the same thing: it had fallen.

Then the doctor came, and the police, and the police did not say the same thing at all.

Three people were still in the theatre that evening.
Each one told the police where they were after seven o'clock, and each one calls that an {{< word def="a reason why you could not have done it, because you were somewhere else at the time" >}}alibi{{< /word >}}.

Two of them are telling the truth.

{{< clues >}}

1. The doctor said Mr Pym had been lying on that stage for about an hour when he was found at half past seven.
2. The rope that holds the sandbag was still tied to the rail above the stage. The knot was neat, and it was not broken.
3. From the end of the show until seven o'clock, Deb Rye stood in the front hall, signing programmes for a long queue of children.
4. From the end of the show until seven o'clock, Vic Lund played the piano while the last people left. Two ladies waiting for a lift sat and listened to him the whole time.
5. Mr Pym's hat and coat were still hanging on his peg by the stage door.

{{< /clues >}}

{{< solution culprit="Alf Corben" >}}

Start with the rope.

When a sandbag falls, it takes its rope down with it, or it leaves a snapped end swinging.
This rope was still tied to the rail, in a neat knot, with nothing broken.
So the sandbag never fell.
Somebody carried it down the ladder and laid it on Mr Pym, so that everybody would look up instead of looking round.

Now the clock.
Mr Pym was found at half past seven, and the doctor said he had been there about an hour.
That means it happened at about **half past six**, when the show had only just ended.

So every alibi for after seven o'clock is worth nothing.
Vic Lund looks like the guilty one, walking to the chip shop with nobody beside him — but he is walking through the wrong hour.

Go back to half past six.

Deb Rye was in the front hall with a queue of children in front of her, signing programmes until seven.

Vic Lund was at the piano until seven, and two ladies sat and listened to every note.

That leaves Alf Corben, alone at the stage door, with nobody to say where he was.

And Alf told everybody that Mr Pym said goodnight at seven o'clock.
A man who is saying goodnight and walking home is wearing his coat.
Mr Pym's coat and hat never left their peg.
Alf made up that goodnight to push the whole evening an hour late, so that his own quiet hour at the door would not matter.

His {{< word def="the reason somebody had for doing it" >}}motive{{< /word >}} was in the door book.
Mr Pym had caught Alf letting people in at the stage door for a coin in his own pocket, and on Monday he was going to tell the manager.
