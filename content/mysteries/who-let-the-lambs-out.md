---
title: Who Let the Lambs Out?
date: 2026-08-07
setting: Wren Farm
incident: Six lambs let out of a shut pen
difficulty: 3
---

The six new lambs live in a pen inside the barn.
The pen gate is held shut by a rope. A lamb cannot untie a rope.

Hal shut the pen at **nine o'clock**. On his way out he set a sack of feed down by the door.
The sack split, and the pellets spread right across the doorway behind him.
After that, anyone who walked into the barn had to tread on them.

At **six in the morning** the pen was empty and the six lambs were up in the top field.

All three of these people were at Wren Farm in the night, and each one has an {{< word def="a reason why you could not have done it, because you were somewhere else at the time" >}}alibi{{< /word >}}.
Two of them are telling the truth.

{{< suspects >}}

{{< suspect name="Hal Tumm" role="The Shepherd" emoji="🐑" >}}
"I shut the pen at nine and went straight to bed. I never came back."
{{< /suspect >}}

{{< suspect name="Ivy Renn" role="The Tractor Driver" emoji="🚜" >}}
"I came out to mend the tractor when the rain started at eleven. I worked till one."
{{< /suspect >}}

{{< suspect name="Bram Ost" role="The Milkman" emoji="🥛" >}}
"I got here at four for the churns. I saw the lambs up in the top field."
{{< /suspect >}}

{{< /suspects >}}

{{< clues >}}

1. It rained hard from eleven o'clock until one. Before eleven the yard was dry. After it, the yard was thick mud.
2. One line of crushed pellets leads from the barn door to the pen gate, and back to the door again.
3. There is no mud anywhere on that line.
4. Ivy's boots were caked with mud. Her prints stop just inside the barn door, where she stood out of the rain.
5. The top gate, where the lambs got out, was propped open with a shepherd's {{< word def="a long stick with a hook at the top, that a shepherd uses" >}}crook{{< /word >}}.

{{< /clues >}}

{{< solution culprit="Hal Tumm" >}}

Start with the pellets.

They only spread across the doorway at nine o'clock, so the crushed line was made **after nine**.
And there is no mud on it — so it was made **before eleven**, when the yard turned to mud.

Somebody walked to the pen gate and back between nine and eleven. That is the whole case.

Ivy did not come out until eleven, when the rain started. Her boots show it: they are caked with mud, and her prints stop just inside the door where she sheltered. She never crossed to the pen.

Bram did not arrive until four in the morning. By then the yard was mud, and he would have carried it in with him. There is none.

That leaves Hal, who says he shut the pen at nine and never came back — but somebody walked to that gate after nine, while the yard was still dry, and he was the only person on the farm.

The crook holding the top gate open is his.

His {{< word def="the reason someone had for doing it" >}}motive{{< /word >}} was the barn roof.
It drips right over the pen, and Hal saw the storm coming.
He moved the lambs up to the dry field to save them a soaking — and then said nothing, because he had promised to mend that roof a month ago and never had.

{{< /solution >}}
